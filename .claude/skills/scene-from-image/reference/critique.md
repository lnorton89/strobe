# Critique checklist

Look at the photo and the frames side by side and answer each line with what
you see. Write the differences as a list. "Looks good" is not an answer.

## Composition and scale

- Does the hero fill the height it fills in the painting? Are the left and
  right thirds alive, with marks of the same family, not empty planks?
- Is the reading order the painting's (what you see first, second, third)?
- Any large empty corner the painting does not have?

## The marks

- Stroke width compared with the photo, at the same scale. Too thin and
  feathery is the commonest fault.
- Do strokes look hand-made (taper, pressure, wobble, overshoot) or like
  vector clip art, stamps, icons or emoji?
- Is repetition too regular: wallpaper, combs, rulers, barber poles,
  identical copies?
- Are dense marks merging into blobs, or glow piling into haze?

## Material and colour

- Is each material read correctly (paint pen, chalk, foil, wood, bone)?
- Anything neon, glossy-tube, plastic, bronze or CG-bevelled that the
  painting is not?
- Do the paint colours match the paint (not the photo's room light)? White
  ink drifting lavender, gold going orange, sun going muddy.

## Technical

- Glow clipped into rectangles or seams at cell or bounds edges.
- Aliasing or moire in fine patterns; 2x2 blocks.
- At 2, 7, 15, 40 and 300 s: anything half-drawn, frozen, jumping, or the
  same at every moment?
- Every control at both extremes: does it change something you can see, and
  does nothing break?

## Motion and music

- Does what moves make sense for the subject?
- With the synthetic beat: do kicks push, does bass swell, do highs sparkle,
  without strobing?

## Character

- Would the painter recognise their piece's feel: cartoonish, game-like,
  neon or generic where the painting is not?
